# Hackriculture YouTube content plan for The Vegetable Guru

Prepared 19 October 2026. Proposed direction for John to review.

Use the channel's practical gardening demonstrations to show why a well-organised reference book is useful. Keep solving real viewer problems, then show the relevant crop guide, troubleshooting spread or technique in *The Vegetable Guru*. Begin with two main videos a month, supported by clips from the same filming. Release announcements should follow physical-proof approval and a working purchase page.

## Subjects to develop

The book's three useful entry points are its 44 vegetable guides, 14 Troubles guides and Growing Techniques section. Match videos to those jobs: grow a crop, identify a problem, or plan the next task. The current [book contents](/Users/johnlennon/Documents/web_site/hackriculture-data/book-layout/contents.json) include compost, plot planning, cultivation, sowing, watering and greenhouses.

For later spring and summer films, return to outdoor tomatoes, watering decisions and climbing-crop supports. Harvest-to-kitchen videos remain valuable expressions of self-sufficiency, but describe the book as a growing companion: do not imply that it contains a recipe or preservation collection. Label observations from Cornwall so viewers elsewhere can judge their conditions.

## A manageable release sequence

For preparation beginning this autumn, make the winter-plot and compost episodes first. Use winter for small-plot planning and the crop-guide demonstration. Move sowing, slug and tomato episodes to the locally relevant growing season. Let actual garden conditions determine the subject rather than forcing a fixed calendar.

Over the first six fortnights, aim for six main videos, with one useful Short from each and a second only when the footage warrants it. Reserve the book demonstration for the release window; a clearly labelled proof preview can come earlier. Ask one specific question in each main video, such as which crop viewers want explained next, then use actual responses to choose a follow-up. Avoid making a weekly livestream or daily posting schedule necessary for the launch.

Open with the gardening problem and show the result or decision. Once the viewer has received something useful, spend roughly 15–25 seconds showing the matching book spread. Close with one clear next step. Keep broader DIY and energy uploads within Hackriculture's identity; make their book mentions occasional and relevant.

## Links and evidence of success

Give each main video one primary destination: its relevant growing page for practical crop help, or `/book/` for book demonstrations. Place it near the top of the description and repeat it in a pinned comment after confirming the channel's advanced-feature access. Official [YouTube link guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639?hl=en) requires that access for clickable external links in long-video descriptions/comments. It also confirms that Shorts description and comment URLs are not clickable. Route Shorts through a profile link or an eligible [related full video](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en), which also requires advanced-feature access.

Use distinct campaign links to distinguish videos. Review results after four to six main releases: relevant website visits, sample interest and outbound purchase clicks, alongside actual Bookvault orders where available. A purchase click is not a sale. Establish the first baseline before setting numerical sales targets; compare videos at similar ages. If visitors arrive but do not continue to the shop, improve the sample, book explanation or purchasing information before increasing production.

The desired result is a repeatable path from useful demonstration to relevant advice, visible book quality and a clear purchase choice, sustained by a filming rhythm John can maintain.
