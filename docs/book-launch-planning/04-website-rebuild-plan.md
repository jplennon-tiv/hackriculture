# Hackriculture website rebuild plan for The Vegetable Guru

Prepared for John Lennon on 19 October 2026. Proposal for the existing hackriculture.com website.

Make Hackriculture the useful, familiar meeting point between John's videos and The Vegetable Guru. The main sales journey should be **a useful YouTube demonstration → a convincing book page with readable samples → the correct Bookvault checkout**. Keep the growing guides freely available; the book offers an organised, illustrated reference to keep and consult through the season.

The book is awaiting its first physical proof. The working price is £17.50, and there is no public purchase URL yet. The website can be prepared now, with its buying action activated when the edition, price and listing are settled. The [current book handover](../../../hackriculture-print/docs/handover/START-HERE.md) remains authoritative for publication status.

John has now authorised a local pre-launch rebuild and selected the **Terracotta Feature layout with the Harvest & Ink palette**: a large golden book lead on the left, dark olive text and buttons, compact channel videos on the right and a pale gold launch form directly beneath the book. His 20 October direction removes the front-page crop-guide grid and emphasises the book and videos. Hackriculture's identity must include food, DIY and home energy as well as gardening. The [selected preview](../prelaunch-designs/concept.html?design=clay&palette=harvest) is the visual direction for implementation; both layout and colour are settled. The first-proof video should follow inspection of the copy, with the pre-launch page and tested sign-up journey ready before the video is published.

## Strategy behind the layout

The [marketing report](01-marketing-plan.md) makes demonstration and existing audience trust the basis of the launch. The [YouTube report](02-youtube-content-plan.md) connects practical garden problems, seasonal projects and affordable growing to relevant book material. The [website goals](03-website-goals.md) turn those recommendations into a clear visitor journey and measurable outcomes.

The current [homepage](https://hackriculture.com/) presents Hackriculture's broader self-sufficiency work and invites visitors to browse guides or watch YouTube. Retain that useful identity, but give the book the leading position during launch. Keep DIY, energy and preserving in the video library; give gardening the strongest prominence on pages promoting this book.

Send a book demonstration video straight to the book page. Send a crop tutorial to its matching guide when that is the resource promised in the video, then offer a relevant book sample. Avoid a generic homepage detour for someone already asking to see the book.

## Pages and navigation

Use five main navigation destinations: **Home · The book · Growing guides · Videos · About John**. The logo returns home. On supporting pages, a prominent “Explore the book” link goes to the book page; on that page, the leading action changes with availability. Place YouTube and utility links in the footer as well.

A future reader-resources page can follow if purchasers repeatedly need material that the existing guides do not cover.

## Homepage layout

The homepage should introduce the book within the first screen while remaining recognisably Hackriculture.

1. **Compact masthead.** Existing logo, newspaper typography and navigation. Explicitly identify growing, food, DIY and home energy as Hackriculture subjects.
2. **Prominent book launch.** Use the selected large golden lead feature on the left, with dark olive text and buttons from the Harvest & Ink palette. Show the real cover, short benefit, honest availability and an ungated sample action. The compact video column sits alongside on desktop.
3. **Early launch notification.** Place the optional email form beside or immediately below the book feature. Connect and test the real provider only in the implementation stage.
4. **Videos and projects.** Give selected videos strong early placement. Include gardening, preserving and home-energy/DIY subjects; do not imply that an older curated video is a new upload. Compact story rows or cards should lead to YouTube.
5. **Browse the wider channel.** Provide subject links for garden/allotment, plot-to-plate and DIY/home energy. Growing guides remain available through navigation and the footer; do not place the crop-guide grid on the front page.
6. **Look inside.** Show actual book spreads with enlargement, leading to the dedicated book page once implemented.
7. **Meet John.** Use a real portrait and the broader home, garden, making, repairing and energy story.
8. **Closing invitation and footer.** Repeat the book invitation once, then provide guide, YouTube, contact and privacy links.

## Design direction

John's 20 October selection supersedes the earlier spacious handbook proposal. Use a **compact self-sufficiency newspaper**, combining the Terracotta Feature layout with Harvest & Ink colours: a golden book lead (#e0bb56), dark olive actions (#303b2b), a pale gold form (#f8edcd), and warm near-white paper (#fcfbf7). Retain the Hackriculture logo, strong masthead, genuine Cornwall photographs, clear column rules and modest serif headlines. Book and video content replace the front-page crop index.

Keep text comfortable to read while using tighter section spacing and useful columns. On the homepage, place the book introduction, launch form and selected videos close together; use enlargement for reading the actual book pages. On the dedicated book page, retain a clear cover, availability and readable samples. Use real photographs of the physical book once John is satisfied with the proof. Avoid imagery suggesting a waterproof or flat-opening product: the present edition is a conventional perfect-bound paperback.

On mobile, stack content in reading order, keep controls comfortably tappable, and place purchase information near each purchase button. Keep samples independently enlargable. Use visible keyboard focus, descriptive image text and strong text contrast. Video thumbnails can continue opening YouTube; the first screen should load without a video player, large PDF or third-party tracking dependency.

## Availability and Bookvault handoff

| State | Website action | Supporting information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparing for release | “Look inside the book” and “Email me when it’s available” | Explain that the first printed proof is under review. Offer an optional launch notification; publish a date only when settled. |
| Ready to sell | “Buy at [verified storefront name]” | Show the confirmed price, actual edition and clear delivery wording. Say that checkout takes place on the named external store. |
| Temporarily unavailable | “Look inside” and accurate availability text | Keep the page useful and remove the active purchase action until the destination is usable again. |

Bookvault's [Buy Now documentation](https://help.bookvault.app/how-to-create-buy-now-links-in-bookvault) describes a hosted purchase route. The final button must use the link generated for the existing title, and its label must match the store the customer actually sees. Verify the final destination, title, cover, edition, retail price, delivery choices and order-support route. Do not substitute the physical-proof postage for a customer delivery quote or assume that a different Bookvault sales channel has identical fees.

An intent-driven buyer should need one purchase click from the book page to reach the retailer's relevant product or cart. Provide a normal visible link; do not insert an unnecessary intermediate “leaving this website” screen. The retailer owns payment and order confirmation. Only promise a return link or website confirmation page if the actual checkout supports it.

## Outcomes and measurement

These are proposed acceptance criteria and review questions, not sales forecasts.

| Outcome | What success looks like | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitors understand the offer | Three informal readers can identify the book's audience, purpose, format and next step after briefly viewing the page. | Ask them to explain it in their own words on a phone. |
| Visitors can inspect the product | All three extracts are readable and enlargable without registration. | Check a narrow phone screen and a desktop, including keyboard use. |
| Purchase intent reaches the right place | Every active purchase button reaches the same correct edition and consistent price; delivery information is easy to find. | Follow every distinct route before launch and after changing the listing. |
| Existing advice remains useful | All 44 approved crop URLs still work, with their approved prose preserved. | Check routes, content preservation and representative guide layouts during the rebuild. |
| John learns which videos generate interest | A weekly record distinguishes video-tagged arrivals, sample interactions, retailer clicks and actual retailer orders. | Use a small set of campaign links and minimal measurement if introduced; report counts as well as rates. |
| The site remains manageable | One place controls book status, price, edition and purchase link; routine launch review takes about 30 minutes a week. | Try the update process and review the time after the first month. |

For the first 30 days on sale, establish a baseline rather than promise a conversion percentage. If the site gains measurement, record **book-page sessions with a purchase click ÷ measured book-page sessions** and **sessions opening a sample ÷ measured book-page sessions**, alongside their actual counts. Report the number of verified retailer orders separately. An outbound click is purchase intent, not a sale; do not claim video-by-video sales attribution unless the retailer supplies it reliably. Where measurement is absent, report a gap rather than an estimated result.

Review the journey in order. Low arrivals suggest the video invitation or link placement needs attention. Arrivals with little sample interest suggest the introduction or sample access needs improvement. Retailer clicks with few orders are a prompt to inspect price, delivery and checkout friction, while recognising that orders and site visits may not be fully attributable.

## Rebuild sequence

1. **Prepare the essentials while the proof is pending.** Select a new visual direction, then build the book page and homepage introduction locally; retain guide routes and approved prose. Prepare sample assets, truthful availability text, mobile layouts and the launch-notification form. Test confirmation and unsubscribe before publishing the form. Keep the first release focused on these changes and the shared navigation.
2. **Finish publication details.** John reviews the proof and settles the public edition, cover, metadata, price and release timing. Prepare the existing Bookvault title for public sale and verify the generated purchase route when authorised.
3. **Make the buying journey ready.** Add the confirmed link and delivery wording, check it on desktop and mobile, and prepare the launch video's website link. John handles the website upload.
4. **Launch through the channel.** Use one clear book demonstration, followed by useful seasonal or problem-solving videos that show a relevant page. Aim for two main videos a month and one useful Short from each, using existing filming where practical. Start with no paid advertising. Shorts should point through the channel profile or a related full video because their description and comment URLs are not clickable; see the [YouTube report](02-youtube-content-plan.md) for platform requirements.
5. **Review after 30 days.** Use actual traffic, confirmed launch sign-ups, questions, retailer clicks and orders to choose the next improvement. Add a wider newsletter, extra landing pages or paid promotion only when they solve an observed need and John has capacity to maintain them.
