Beetroot’s sweet, earthy roots suit raw salads, hot dishes, pickles, borsch and chutneys; young leaves are edible
too. This cool-climate crop needs well-drained, humus-rich soil and steady moisture. Growth starts slowly, then
roots swell quickly. Sow small batches or raise module clumps. Early sowings under cover can give golf-ball-
sized roots by late spring; later sowings provide summer, autumn and storage crops. Lift before roots become
woody or leathery. In mild winters some can stay outside, but severe frost can freeze and rot them.
