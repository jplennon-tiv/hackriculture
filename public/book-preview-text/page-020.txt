Parsnips are slow, deep-rooting biennials grown for white tap roots that sweeten as cold turns starch to sugar.
They occupy the ground for a long season and seedlings emerge slowly, but established plants need little
beyond weed control and moisture while young. Use fresh seed: cold or dry soil and sowing too deeply can
leave a blank row. Deep, humus-rich, stone-free ground gives the best roots, preferably undug or improved for
an earlier crop; avoid fresh manure.
