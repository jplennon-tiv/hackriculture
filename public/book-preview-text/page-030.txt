Turnips are faster-growing and less hardy than swedes. Pull them young for mild, juicy spring roots, sow as a
catch crop after summer harvests, or store small autumn roots for winter stews. They usually have white flesh
and leaves arising directly from the root, without a raised neck. Early types take 60–80 days, sometimes almost
as fast as radishes. Autumn roots take longer; eat them before the hardest winter weather. Some varieties also
give edible turnip tops.
