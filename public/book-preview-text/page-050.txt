Leaf beets suit gardeners who enjoy spinach but find it quick to bolt or difficult in dry weather. Swiss chard,
seakale beet, spinach beet and perpetual spinach are grown for leaves, not swollen roots; a few plants give
repeated harvests over a long season. Chard has broad leaves and thick, edible midribs in white, red, yellow or
pink, making it attractive in a flower border. Spinach beet has plainer green leaves, used like spinach. Pick
regularly: young leaves are tender for salads or cooking, while oversized leaves toughen and growth slows. In
mild winters, established plants may give an early-spring flush before flowering.
