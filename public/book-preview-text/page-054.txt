Endive is a cool-season salad plant with more flavour and bitterness than lettuce, though less of lettuce's
crispness. It can be eaten raw in mixed salads or cooked, and both curly-leaved frisée types and broader-leaved
Batavian or escarole types are useful. Curly endive is usually for late-summer and autumn cutting, while broad-
leaved endive is easier to grow for good hearts and is more useful in autumn and winter. The crop needs
moisture, humus and some protection from heat or harsh frost; blanching, or choosing self-blanching varieties,
gives paler, milder hearts. Small late-summer sowings can provide leaves and hearts into winter, with suitable
protection in cold weather.
