Four beds, four years
The acme of rotation is the traditional four-block rotation system, as seen in many an old gardening
manual. It’s prescriptive, but it gives you a clear plan for the whole plot. Divide the growing area into four
beds or groups of beds, then move each crop group on one place each year.

  Year                 Bed A                           Bed B                           Bed C                         Bed D

    1             Peas & beans                       Brassicas                       Potatoes                     Onions & roots

    2                Brassicas                       Potatoes                    Onions & roots                   Peas & beans

    3                Potatoes                    Onions & roots                   Peas & beans                      Brassicas

    4            Onions & roots                   Peas & beans                       Brassicas                      Potatoes

Brassicas follow peas and beans to use nitrogen released from their decaying roots. Read across for one
year, down for one bed. Year 5 repeats Year 1.

   Alternative strategy: A right British muddle
   If you only have a small plot, or you have many different-sized beds, you can just mix plants up in
   small groups and let randomness and Nature do the rotation for you. It will still work; just keep things
   moving, and every year, do something different in each bed.
