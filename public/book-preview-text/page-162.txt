Greenhouses Pots and raised beds
Containers, raised soil beds and floor-level no-dig borders all have a place. The ground beneath
your greenhouse and the crops you want to grow will help you choose.


                                                                        1. Pots and growbags
                                                                        A concrete or paving slab floor gives a firm surface
                                                                        for pots and growbags. Arrange them on the floor
                                                                        or on wooden staging, keeping the doorway and
                                                                        working space clear. Staging must be level and
                                                                        strong enough for the weight of wet compost;
                                                                        leave tall crops on the floor for headroom.

                                                                        Use peat-free potting compost and containers with
                                                                        drainage holes. For tomatoes, choose pots at least
                                                                        12 inches across. Let surplus water drain away, and
                                                                        check pots and bags regularly: their small supply of
           A solid floor with containers above it.                      water is soon used up.


                                                                        2. Raised brick beds
                                                                        Brick beds give roots a larger supply of soil and
                                                                        moisture, with less need for pots and bought-in
                                                                        compost. Plan the beds and path before building
                                                                        the base. Set the frame on top of the bricks to
                                                                        preserve plant headroom inside. Fasten it to the
                                                                        bricks with masonry fittings.

                                                                        Leave the beds open to the earth below. If building
                                                                        work has compacted it, loosen it with a fork before
                                                                        filling with topsoil and mature compost. Thereafter,
                                                                        dig compost in or add it at the surface for no-dig
                                                                        growing. Keep the path clear and the soil within

   If roots run into trouble
   If root disease develops, identify it before reusing the ground. Rotate where practical, or use
   containers with fresh compost; for raised beds, exchange the soil for fresh garden soil and compost.
